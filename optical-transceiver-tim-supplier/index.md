## Choose the qualification route

| Project situation | First engineering action | Decision before samples or RFQ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New module design | Map heat sources, housing path, gap stack and force budget | Select a suitable TIM family and target thickness range |
| Existing material replacement | Benchmark incumbent and candidate under matched conditions | Decide whether the candidate advances to module testing |
| Second-source qualification | Freeze the incumbent reference, finished-part drawing and acceptance gates | Define sample, multi-lot, reliability and pilot-build requirements |
| 800G to 1.6T platform change | Recheck power distribution, form factor, housing and host boundary | Determine which earlier evidence can be reused and what must be revalidated |

## Define the installed interface before contacting suppliers

Provide a sanitized input package where confidentiality permits:

- module form factor and heat-source locations;
- contact-zone dimensions, keep-outs and surface materials;
- minimum, nominal and maximum gap after tolerance stack-up;
- local and total allowable contact force;
- relevant case, component or interface-temperature criterion;
- electrical, cleanliness, volatile, silicone or compatibility restrictions where applicable;
- host cage, heat sink or riding-heat-sink boundary;
- assembly, liner removal, inspection, rework and service expectations.

The labels 400G, 800G and 1.6T do not define one universal pad thickness, hardness or conductivity. Use the applicable form-factor specification and the actual hardware drawing.

## Compare the parameters that control installed performance

| Variable | Why it matters | What evidence to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thermal conductivity | Method-dependent material property used for screening | Original method, specimen direction, temperature and typical-versus-guaranteed status |
| Thermal resistance | Better reflects the tested interface than conductivity alone | Thickness, pressure, surfaces, temperature and fixture details |
| Bond-line or compressed thickness | Directly changes bulk resistance and contact behavior | Nominal and tolerance limits at representative assembly conditions |
| Compression and force | Too little can leave poor contact; too much can load the package, PCB or housing | Compression-deflection evidence across the required gap range |
| Contact resistance | Can dominate a thin, small interface | Same-surface and same-pressure comparison with the incumbent |
| Cleanliness and compatibility | Residue, particles or material interaction may affect assembly risk | Defined test, handling, packaging and inspection controls |
| Finished-part control | Sheet data does not prove die-cut consistency | Dimensions, edges, liner, orientation, lot traceability and converting-site controls |
| Reliability | Contact and material behavior may change with aging | Application-relevant cycling, humidity, vibration or insertion exposure |

## Benchmark and qualification

Use this gated path:

1. **Requirement definition:** freeze the heat path, tolerance stack, force budget and acceptance criteria.
2. **Document screening:** align methods, units, thickness and test conditions; identify missing evidence.
3. **Same-condition benchmark:** compare incumbent and candidate at matched compressed thickness, pressure, surfaces and temperature.
4. **Finished-part sample:** inspect die cuts, liner, orientation, cleanliness, packaging and handling.
5. **Module and host validation:** test representative modules, cages and heat sinks, including relevant tolerance extremes.
6. **Reliability and multiple lots:** repeat the critical checks after project-relevant exposure and across delivered lots.
7. **Pilot build and supplier qualification:** verify normal operators, placement, inspection, rework, traceability and change control.
8. **RFQ readiness:** freeze the approved material and finished-part revision, quantity, packaging, evidence and commercial scope.

Coupon data supports screening but does not establish module approval. A candidate advances only when the application owner's predefined acceptance gates are met.

## What procurement should freeze before RFQ

- approved material, thickness and finished-part drawing revision;
- manufacturing and converting locations included in the approval scope;
- critical dimensions, tolerances, liner and packaging configuration;
- lot traceability, certificate and incoming-inspection requirements;
- storage, shelf-life and handling controls;
- nonconformance, corrective-action and supplier change-notification rules;
- requalification triggers for formulation, raw material, process, tool, liner or site changes.

## Common sourcing mistakes

1. Ranking candidates by W/m·K without matching thickness and pressure.
2. Designing from nominal gap while ignoring minimum and maximum stack conditions.
3. Approving sheet properties but not the production die cut and liner.
4. Reusing an 800G result for 1.6T without confirming the architecture and force boundary.
5. Testing one sample lot without traceability or change-control terms.
6. Requesting an RFQ before the technical configuration and validation gates are frozen.
