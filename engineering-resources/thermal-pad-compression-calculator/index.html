What this tool calculates

Compression amount is original thickness − compressed BLT. Compression ratio is that amount divided by original thickness. If both conductivity and area are supplied, the tool also calculates theoretical bulk resistance using R = t/(k × A) after converting millimetres and square millimetres to SI units.

Higher compression is not always better

Compression can improve surface conformity and reduce interfacial air, but excessive compression can raise assembly force, bend a PCB, load optical or power components, amplify tolerance-stack risk and affect long-term recovery. Compression suitability must be validated against the specific material’s compression-force curve, tolerance stack-up, component load limit and assembly conditions.

Example and engineering limitations

A 2.0 mm pad assembled into a 1.6 mm gap has 0.4 mm compression and a 20% compression ratio. That arithmetic does not establish suitability. Validate minimum and maximum gap, force-deflection behavior, compressed thermal resistance, contact quality, dimensional tolerance and post-aging condition.
