Answer first

A useful TIM benchmark compares the incumbent and candidate under the same thickness or bond-line thickness, pressure, surfaces, temperature, test method and acceptance criteria. A higher datasheet W/m·K value alone cannot establish a replacement.

Start with the information you already have

You do not need to complete a long RFQ questionnaire. Send one contact method and any two or three items available below:

  • Current material model or TDS
  • Material type: pad, gel, grease, insulator, potting compound or adhesive
  • Application and heat source
  • Nominal, minimum and maximum gap or target BLT
  • The first result to verify: temperature, thermal resistance, insulation, compression, dispensing or reliability

What a controlled comparison should hold constant

Use the same fixture or assembly, contact area, surfaces, gap or BLT, mounting pressure, input power, ambient condition, instrumentation and conditioning. Record the exact material thickness, sample lot and test method. If one condition changes, label the result as exploratory rather than a direct comparison.

For thermal pads, include compression-deflection, hardness method, thickness tolerance and recovery. For gels, include dispense amount, achieved BLT, voids, slump, pump-out or bleed and cure conditions where applicable. For electrical insulators, thermal performance must be reviewed together with the required dielectric evidence and the complete insulation system.

From benchmark to RFQ

  1. Requirement definition: document the installed interface and acceptance criteria.
  2. TDS screening: identify comparable values and evidence gaps.
  3. Bench comparison: test incumbent and candidate under matched conditions.
  4. Application validation: confirm component temperature, mechanics, insulation and process behavior.
  5. Reliability and multiple lots: apply the stresses and consistency checks relevant to the project.
  6. Pilot build: verify production-representative parts, handling and inspection.
  7. Supplier qualification and RFQ: close traceability, change control, lead-time and commercial requirements.

Acceptance criteria belong to the application owner. A successful material-property test does not by itself qualify the assembled product.

Frequently asked questions

Can a higher W/m·K material directly replace the current TIM?

No. Installed resistance also depends on BLT, contact resistance, pressure, flatness, wetting, compression or rheology and aging. Compare the complete interface under matched conditions.

Can I begin without a drawing or complete specification?

Yes. A current TDS, application name and one known gap or problem are enough to begin a private engineering discussion. Sensitive drawings should only be shared through an appropriate controlled process.

Should samples be requested before the benchmark plan is defined?

Initial samples may support screening, but the material form, thickness, quantity and test sequence should reflect the intended interface. Otherwise the sample may not answer the qualification question.

Does a successful benchmark prove production qualification?

No. Production approval may also require reliability exposure, multiple lots, converted-part dimensional control, a pilot build, traceability and supplier change control.
