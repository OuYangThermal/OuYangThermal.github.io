## Applications supported

Relevant applications include OBC and DC/DC assemblies, PCS and ESS power electronics, battery PACK cooling plates, BMS electronics, and industrial power modules. Each has different requirements for gap filling, vertical stability, electrical isolation, dispensing, service temperature, and long-term movement.

## Key engineering requirements

Define gap range, interface area, heat load, target temperature, effective thermal resistance, electrical requirements, dispense path, cure condition for two-part materials, density, pump-out risk, vibration, thermal cycling, and rework expectations. Conductivity values are only comparable when methods and conditions are understood.

## Evaluation and qualification

Screen documents first, then confirm dispense repeatability and achievable BLT. Use representative hardware for thermal and reliability tests. Record material lot, mix ratio, cure, pressure, temperatures, and aging. For second-source work, compare the candidate with the incumbent under identical conditions rather than claiming equivalence from datasheets.

## Dispensing compatibility is part of material qualification

Document package type, dispense equipment, pump technology, hose and valve path, static mixer where applicable, needle/nozzle, shot size, bead geometry and takt time. A gel that performs in a hand-dispensed coupon may not remain stable after production residence time, starts and stops, purge cycles or temperature changes.

For two-part materials, verify ratio by mass or another validated method, mixing quality, working time and cure condition. Do not infer cure from surface appearance alone. For one-part materials, confirm storage conditioning, flow after storage and the supplier-defined cure or non-cure behavior.

## Void, slump and pump-out controls

| Risk | Process evidence | Validation evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Void | Degassing/handling, bead path, dispense interruption | Sectioning, imaging or another approved coverage method |
| Slump | Material conditioning, bead dimensions, wait time | Position before and after assembly/cure |
| Pump-out | Gap, pressure, cycling amplitude and surface condition | Post-cycle position, mass/coverage and thermal response |
| Squeeze-out | Shot size, assembly speed and stop geometry | Contamination and keep-out inspection |
| Incomplete cure | Ratio, mixer, temperature and time | Approved cure indicator and mechanical check |

There is no universal allowable void percentage or pump-out test. Define the relevant heat path, geometry and acceptance limit for the assembly.

## Storage, packaging and multi-lot validation

Record storage temperature, conditioning time, shelf life, opened-container controls and packaging orientation from the current supplier documentation. Check leakage, separation, sedimentation or dispense variation after representative transport and storage. Do not invent a remix or warming procedure; follow the material-specific instruction.

Compare multiple lots using the same equipment and program. Review density or another suitable identity check, dispense mass, bead profile, mix ratio, cure, thermal result and reliability response. Link packaging and raw-material lots to pilot-build results.

## Pilot build and supplier change control

The pilot must include normal operators, production equipment, purge and restart conditions, inspection, rework and realistic wait times. Freeze the approved material revision, package, mix/dispense process and acceptance criteria before RFQ.

Require notification for formulation, filler, package, raw-material source, manufacturing site, mixer, test method or shelf-life changes. A successful sample is permission to continue validation—not proof of mass-production readiness.
